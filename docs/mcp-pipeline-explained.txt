FinTrack MCP Pipeline — Explained in Plain English
====================================================

This walks through the 7 steps in docs/mcp-pipeline-sketch.png using one
running example, so you can see what actually happens to a question as it
moves through the system.

Example question: "How much did I spend on dining out last month?"


STEP 1 — Client
----------------
What's happening: You type your question into the chat box in the app
(ScenarioLab on web, or the mobile app). Your device packages that question
up, along with proof of who you are (a login token), and sends it to
FinTrack's server.

Example: You type "How much did I spend on dining out last month?" and hit
send. The app sends that sentence to the server, tagged with your Google
login.


STEP 2 — Auth Gate
-------------------
What's happening: Before the server does anything else, it checks your
login token is real and not expired or faked. Once confirmed, it pulls out
your verified email address and attaches it to the request — this is the
email that will be used for every database lookup from here on, no matter
what.

Example: The server checks your Google token, confirms it's really you
(atharva@example.com), and stamps that email onto your question internally.
You never see this step — it just guarantees nobody can pretend to be you
or peek at someone else's spending.


STEP 3 — Planner LLM
----------------------
What's happening: An AI model reads your question and figures out, in a
structured way, WHAT you're actually asking for — which table of data,
what kind of calculation, and what filters (like a month or category). It
doesn't answer the question yet — it just writes down a plan for how to
get the answer.

Example: The AI reads "How much did I spend on dining out last month?" and
produces a plan like:
    table: transactions
    operation: sum
    filters: category = "dining", type = "expense"
    month: last month
It's like a translator turning your everyday sentence into a form a
librarian could act on.


STEP 4 — Plan Validator
-------------------------
What's happening: Before the plan is allowed to touch any real data, a
strict checker looks it over. It only allows a small set of safe
operations (sum, average, count, top categories, etc.) on a small set of
known tables (transactions, goals, budgets). If the plan asks for anything
outside that — or tries to sneak in someone else's email — it gets
rejected. It also throws away whatever email the AI plan might have
guessed and replaces it with your real, verified email from 
Step 2.

Example: The plan from Step 3 (sum of dining expenses, last month) is
checked — "sum" is allowed, "transactions" is allowed, the filters make
sense — so it's approved, and your verified email is locked in. If the AI
had instead produced something odd, like "delete all transactions" or
"show me bob@email.com's spending," this step would block it and the
system would just ask you to rephrase your question instead of guessing.


STEP 5 — Query Executor
--------------------------
What's happening: Now that the plan is approved, the server runs one exact,
pre-written type of database lookup — no AI guesswork involved, just plain
arithmetic on your real records.

Example: The server runs the equivalent of "add up every expense
transaction where category = dining and date is in last month" against
your actual transaction history, and gets back a real number — say,
$187.40.


STEP 6 — Narrator LLM
------------------------
What's happening: A second AI step takes that real number (and your
original question) and turns it into a natural, helpful sentence — the
kind of reply a financial advisor might give, not just a bare number.

Example: Given "$187.40" and "How much did I spend on dining out last
month?", the AI writes something like: "You spent $187.40 on dining out
last month. That's about 12% of your total spending for the month."


STEP 7 — Response
--------------------
What's happening: The finished answer is sent back to the app and shown
to you in the chat window.

Example: You see FinTrack's reply appear on screen: "You spent $187.40 on
dining out last month. That's about 12% of your total spending for the
month."


THE SAFETY VALVE (dashed path in the diagram)
------------------------------------------------
If Step 4 rejects the plan — say you asked something the system doesn't
know how to turn into a safe query — it skips Steps 5 and 6 entirely and
sends back a short message asking you to rephrase, instead of letting the
AI make something up or touch the database with an unchecked request.
